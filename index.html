01 / WHO WE ARE

We are building the energy layer for the region's electric future.

ZEEN is a Singapore and Vietnam company. We design the battery, the bike, the station and the software as one connected system, so that changing energy is faster than filling a tank and simpler than charging a phone.

Battery swapping only works if one company owns the whole loop. So we build all of it, in house, to one standard.

The region does not need convincing to go electric. It needs energy that keeps up. Charging takes hours riders do not have, home charging is unsafe in dense housing, and the battery is close to half the cost of the bike. Swapping removes all three problems at once.

03 / THE SYSTEM

Four parts, one standard.

THE BIKE

ZEEN X

Built for the riders who work the hardest, the couriers and delivery fleets that cover a hundred and fifty kilometres a day. Two swappable packs under the seat, a connected dashboard, and a frame designed around the swap.

RANGE140 to 200 km on two packs
PACKSTwo, swappable in seconds
DASHBOARDConnected, over the air updates
BUILT FORProfessional daily riders

THE PACK

The cell that moves the city.

An aluminium pack a rider can lift with one hand. Every cell is monitored, every swap logs its health, and any pack that falls out of tolerance is pulled from the network before a rider ever sees it.

ENERGY2.1 kWh
VOLTAGE70 V
CAPACITY30 Ah
WEIGHT12.5 kg

THE STATION

Energy where the city already stops.

Twelve bays that charge, test and balance every pack between riders. A station fits a shopfront, a forecourt, a car park or a lobby, draws ordinary power, and reports its own health continuously.

BAYS12 per station
FOOTPRINTFits a standard shopfront bay
POWERStandard grid supply
MONITORINGLive, pack by pack
SWAP TIMEAbout thirty seconds

THE SOFTWARE

ZEEN OS

The layer that turns hardware into a network. Written in Vietnam, it runs identity, payments, devices, battery intelligence and the routing of energy across every station we operate.

04 / THE NETWORK

The network is the range.
